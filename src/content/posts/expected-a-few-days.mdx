I expected this blog to be a few days of work. Twenty-eight days after the first commit it has one
published post.

The building was the easy part. What sits behind it now is a loop: something that drafts only from
raw material I supply, a critic that returns objections and is forbidden from rewriting a single
line, and a fact-checker that traces every number in a draft back to where it came from. All of that
worked. On my own reckoning it is faster than writing the posts by hand and better than what I would
have produced, because it takes the tedious work away and it does more than I would have done.

What it does not take is the half with my name on it — deciding what I am happy to publish, and
whether it is true.

That half is not small. The rules and the skills that govern how a post gets written run to 10,873
words. The one published post is 2,557. Before either of those mattered I had to hand over 6,552
words of raw material: what happened, in what order, with the numbers and the logs. A drafter that
invents nothing can only arrange what it is given.

Where that supply runs out, the machinery stops rather than guesses. The second post I started is
still where it stopped. It was drafted the day after the site went up, it runs to 1,614 words, and it
carries thirteen open questions. Its central section, the one the format makes mandatory, is this in
full:

Six questions and no prose. Every one of them is answerable, and every one of them is answerable only
by me.

***

The catch is not the iterating. It is that iterating needs direction, and direction competes with
everything else that wants attention. That post has sat untouched for twenty-seven days. In the same
twenty-seven days, forty-five commits landed on this repository across seven working days, including
the drafting and publishing of a different post. I was not away. This blog is not important, so it
was easy to side-line.
