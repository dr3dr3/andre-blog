# LinkedIn

How posts get shared. LinkedIn is the only distribution channel, and its native register is the
exact opposite of this blog's. Resist it.

Everything in [WRITING.md](WRITING.md) applies here unchanged — the kill-list, the voice rules, the
evidence rules, the anonymisation rules. A LinkedIn post is writing, not marketing collateral, and
there is no separate looser standard for it.

## The shape

**Write 200–300 words that stand alone as a complete thought.** Take the single most interesting
insight from the post and deliver it whole — the observation, and what makes it true.

The test: **it must be worth reading even if nobody clicks.** Someone who reads the LinkedIn post
and never opens the blog should have got something out of it. If the post only makes sense as an
advertisement for the article, it is not finished.

This means giving away the good part. Do that. The people who want the full story will read it, and
the people who don't were never going to.

## Rules

- **Never post a bare link with "new blog post 👇".**
- **The link goes in the first comment, or at the end.** Never in the opening line.
- **One post per article.** Do not repost the same article later with different framing. If an
  article is worth two posts, it was two articles.
- Open the same way the article does: a specific noun from the actual story, in the first sentence.
  The first-40-words rule from WRITING.md §5 applies here and matters more, because LinkedIn
  truncates.

## Banned

Hook formatting, all of it:

- One-line paragraphs stacked for the algorithm
- `🧵` and thread markers
- `Here's what I learned:`
- `Most engineers get this wrong.`
- `I'll say the quiet part out loud.`
- A list of one-word lines with an emoji on each
- Asking a question at the end to farm comments
- `Agree?` · `Thoughts?` · `What's been your experience?`
- Tagging people who were not involved

Also banned, as everywhere: the kill-list in WRITING.md §4, exclamation marks, and any adjective
grading the work.

## Emoji

None. Not in the body, not as bullets, not as a pointer to the link.

## Hashtags

At most two, at the end, and only where they are genuinely the name of the thing —
`#platformengineering` is a topic, `#innovation` is noise. None at all is a fine answer.

## Before posting

1. Read it back as though someone else wrote it. Would you keep reading past line two?
2. Cut the first sentence. It is usually throat-clearing. If the post still works, leave it cut.
3. Check the word count. Under 200 is probably a bare link with extra steps; over 300 belongs on the
   blog.
4. Confirm no company name appears, including in any screenshot.
5. Confirm every number in it also appears in the post, and is right.
